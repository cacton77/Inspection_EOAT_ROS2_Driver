Control

Each jog is bounded by the step budget and stops itself. The node re-publishes the command at 20 Hz to hold off the firmware's 500 ms deadman — if this page or the network drops, the lens stops on its own.

Continuous velocity

Full scale is the Velocity setting above, so this is −100…+100% of it. The response is deliberately cubic: at 16 microsteps 200 steps/s is only 3.75 RPM, so a linear taper would waste most of the travel on speeds too fast to place focus with. The centre is a deadzone that snaps to exactly 0.

Releasing springs back to zero, and the release is caught on pointer-up, pointer-cancel, tab switch and page hide. If every one of those is missed the firmware's own deadman still stops the lens within 500 ms — that backstop is why this control is safe to hold. While UNCALIBRATED there are no soft limits, so a sustained hold will reach the mechanical ends; that is what makes it useful for finding them.

Range calibration

Manual stand-in for homing. Jog to the minimum and set it — that position becomes step 0 — then jog to the maximum, back off one jog, and set that. Once both are set the firmware clamps every move to the range inside the step ISR, so the limits hold even if this page or the network dies.

The two right-hand swatches are the single-pixel colour and the NeoKey PS key. Mag+/Mag− are not settable here — the firmware drives them from lens position, so anything sent for them is ignored.

Per-ring and single-pixel are bring-up instruments: they check that the inner/mid/outer boundaries and the chain order in led.cpp match the hardware. That mapping assumes pixel 0 of every ring is physically at 0 radians, which has never been verified against the real rings.

"on strand" is read back from /led_ring/state, so it is what the MCU says it actually rendered. It legitimately differs from the command while homing blanks the ring or a capture owns it — that is information, not a fault.

Brightness is clamped in firmware by LED_RING_MAX_BRIGHTNESS: all 75 pixels at full white would be ~4.5 A at 5 V unclamped. Unlike a jog there is no deadman here — a light is meant to stay on when this page closes.

A stall is declared when sg_result drops below SGTHRS while moving. Jog into free travel first to see the unloaded baseline, then approach a limit slowly and watch where it falls. Pick a threshold comfortably below the free-running value and above the noise floor.

Click or drag on the ring to place the spot. Dots are drawn at each pixel's real computed position and coloured from /led_ring/state — so this is the hardware's readback at the model's coordinates, not a local simulation. If the lit arc appears mirrored about the vertical axis, the ring_clockwise parameter is wrong for how the strands were routed.

Index 0 of every ring points down. The dashed circle is 1σ.

Flux is total, not peak: narrowing σ concentrates the same light rather than also dimming, so width and exposure stay independent. They are not independently unbounded though — concentrating a large flux into few pixels saturates them, and past that the requested total cannot be met. "max flux at this σ" is where that starts.

σ is floored at ~10 mm, which is half the 20.4 mm inner-ring pixel pitch. Below that the spot's apparent width swings by over 100% as it moves — collapsing onto one pixel at some angles and straddling two at others. That is worse than dimming would be, because nothing about the brightness reveals it.
